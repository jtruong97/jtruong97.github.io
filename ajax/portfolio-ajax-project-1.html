Why I built it

I kept finishing the last published book in a series, hitting a cliffhanger, and only then realizing the series was unfinished — sometimes abandoned years earlier. I tracked what I was reading in a notes app, which made it easy to lose a series entirely. Researching whether something was complete before starting took real effort and was often wrong.

So the app answers it directly. It will tell you, for instance, that A Song of Ice and Fire is still ongoing, that the last book landed in 2012, and that nothing has been announced in the fourteen years since.

On your phone

Installs to the home screen and runs full-screen — no app store needed. On iPhone, open fully-bookd.com in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open the browser menu and choose Install app.

Under the hood

Keeping ~15,000 titles current

  • Scheduled Supabase Edge Functions discover new titles from followed authors and backfill missing metadata, using bounded batches, oldest-first queue rotation and a rate-limit circuit breaker so runs resume where they stopped.
  • Metadata reconciled across Hardcover, Google Books and Open Library with per-field fallback, validating candidate publication dates against an independently-sourced anchor year rather than last-write-wins. Field-level locks protect manual corrections, and a 0–100 quality score sets refresh cadence.

Search, dedup and access

  • Local-first search queries the Postgres catalog through a fuzzy-match RPC before falling back to external APIs, with coverage-weighted relevance ranking and series-sibling awareness — so a series name surfaces its sequels. Debounced, TTL-cached and guarded against stale responses.
  • Catalog deduplication automated in PL/pgSQL via pg_cron with per-step logging. Dry runs against production data caught three matching rules with 85–99% false positives before any destructive merge.
  • OAuth 2.0 and JWT auth with row-level security, making shelves, ratings and reviews private by default.
