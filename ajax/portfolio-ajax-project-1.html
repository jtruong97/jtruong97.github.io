Why I built it

I kept finishing the last published book in a series, hitting a cliffhanger, and only then realizing the series was unfinished — sometimes abandoned years earlier. I tracked what I was reading in a notes app, which made it easy to lose a series entirely, and researching whether something was complete took real effort and was often wrong.

So the app answers it directly, and lets you filter a reading list down to series that have actually finished.

Under the hood

Scheduled Supabase Edge Functions keep ~15,000 titles current — discovering releases from followed authors and backfilling metadata in bounded batches, with oldest-first queue rotation and a rate-limit circuit breaker so runs resume where they stopped. Metadata is reconciled across three external APIs with per-field fallback, validating publication dates against an independently-sourced anchor year rather than last-write-wins. Search hits the Postgres catalog through a fuzzy-match RPC before falling back to those APIs, ranked so a series name surfaces its sequels. Deduplication runs in PL/pgSQL via pg_cron, where dry runs against production data caught three matching rules with 85–99% false positives before any destructive merge.
