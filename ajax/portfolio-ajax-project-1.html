Why I built it

I kept finishing the last published book in a series, hitting a cliffhanger, and only then realizing the series was unfinished — sometimes abandoned years earlier. I tracked what I was reading in a notes app, which made it easy to lose a series entirely, and researching whether something was complete took real effort and was often wrong.

So the app answers it directly, and lets you filter a reading list down to series that have actually finished.

Under the hood

The catalog grows itself. A nightly cron adds new titles and keeps existing ones accurate — that pipeline is most of the work.

  • Supabase Edge Functions run nightly on a cron, discovering new releases and backfilling metadata in bounded batches, with a rate-limit circuit breaker so runs resume where they stopped.
  • Three external APIs reconciled per-field, validating dates against an anchor year rather than last-write-wins.
  • Search hits Postgres through a fuzzy-match RPC before falling back to those APIs, ranked so a series name surfaces its sequels.
  • Deduplication in PL/pgSQL via pg_cron — dry runs caught three rules with 85–99% false positives before any destructive merge.
